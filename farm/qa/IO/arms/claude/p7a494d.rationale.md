# p7a494d: ABSTAIN

**Decision:** keep the tool's pins for every group.

**Why**
- Every one of the worst setup paths (WS 5.70 ns at T0) starts at one flop, i1330/Q. That flop fans out to all 64 output bits (G01, G05) through a 3-level buf_2 chain (max fanout 10), then a per-bit buf_2 > buf_1 > clkbuf_4 > pin. Cell delays dominate; the pin wire adds about 0.003 ns.
- The chain shape (9 bits plus the next buffer per level) comes from the tool's greedy fanout repair. With 64 bits on two sides of the driver, 3 levels is the floor. The stock line is already balanced (6 direct, 28 on one side, 30 on the other). A single line on W or S keeps 3 levels. Splitting G01 and G05 across two edges lengthens the root net and removes no level.
- G02, G04 and G06 are unconnected, G03 is a tie cell, and G07 has about 7 ns of slack. Register-to-register paths are set by hold delay cells, not by pins.

**Alternatives considered** (effect on the worst output arrival; confidence the move helps)
- G01 and G05 to W as one line: about 0 ± 0.05 ns, and it may crowd the logic near CLK, RST and G07. Confidence 0.3.
- G02 and G04 to W, then G01 and G05 to S1/S2: the trunk is about 20% shorter, maybe -0.03 ns. A deeper bottom margin and more crowding offset part of that, and S may not fit in later builds. Confidence 0.4.
- G01 or G05 alone to W (outputs split between E and W): +0.05 to +0.1 ns, i.e. worse. Confidence 0.15.

No move is expected to gain more than build-to-build noise, so abstaining carries the least risk.
